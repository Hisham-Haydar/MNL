# JMP Literature-Review Skeleton — v1

**Intended location:** `docs/JMP_literature_review_skeleton_v1.md`
**Document class:** writing scaffold. Claims, source groups, overclaim warnings, and paragraph plans. **Not** final prose; no polished literature-review paragraphs are written here.
**Sources used:** `JMP_project_state_v1.md` (RQ, contribution, boundaries, certified baseline); `JMP_welfare_spec_v5.md` (W¹–W⁶ family, Exercise A/B); `INDEX_02`–`INDEX_07` (source evidence); `INDEX_08_writing_bank_v2` (paragraph skeletons §§1–16); `INDEX_01_v2` (bibliographic organization and citation-warning queue only).
**Discipline:** access / ability / preference three-way cut throughout (never the obsolete two-way opportunity/preference split). Wage technology = ability; market/job/hours/region/occupation availability = access. Occupation availability is access, never preference. W¹–W⁶ is the JMP's welfare design, not a literature object; sources support its components only by analogy.

---

## 1. Purpose

To convert the accepted literature indexes into a section-by-section writing scaffold for the JMP literature review, so that prose drafting can proceed claim-by-claim with each claim already tied to its source group, its overclaim warning, and its position in the section architecture. The scaffold fixes (a) what each literature block must establish, (b) which sources carry each claim and at what strength (explicit / background / derived-by-analogy), (c) where the JMP departs from the literature, and (d) which claims are citation-ready versus blocked on metadata repair. It deliberately stops short of final prose so that the positioning decisions are reviewable before any paragraph is committed.

---

## 2. Proposed literature-review role in the JMP

The review does one job: locate the JMP at the intersection of four literatures that have never been combined around a single estimated opportunity object — latent-jobs/RURO structural labour supply, random-utility money-metric welfare, responsibility-sensitive equivalent income and equality of opportunity, and Shapley–Shorrocks decomposition — and show that each supplies a *component* the JMP integrates, while none supplies the integration. The review is not a survey; it is a positioning argument whose payoff is the contribution claim in §14. It must also pre-empt three misreadings the project state explicitly guards against: that the paper is a country-ranking exercise, that it is a EUROMOD/microsimulation application, and that it is "just another structural labour-supply paper." Each misreading gets a dedicated defensive paragraph (writing bank §§8, 9, 10).

The review should appear after the introduction and before the model, and should be organized by *function in the JMP* (the eight blocks below), not by chronology or by author school.

---

## 3. Core positioning paragraph

**Claim cluster (the one-paragraph thesis the whole review builds toward):**
The JMP ties a single estimated opportunity density to three layers — structural estimation of labour supply as choice among latent jobs, ex-ante money-metric welfare computed over the household's feasible set, and a Shapley–Shorrocks decomposition of welfare inequality into access, ability, and preference. The literature supplies each layer's machinery separately — latent jobs and identification [Dagsvik & Jia 2016, explicit; Capeau & Decoster 2016, explicit; Beffy et al. 2019, explicit], money-metric random-utility welfare [Bargain et al. 2013, explicit; Dagsvik & Karlström 2005, explicit], and order-independent decomposition [Shorrocks 2013, explicit; Audoly et al. 2025, explicit] — but no source carries one opportunity object consistently through all three. The contribution is that integration and the responsibility-sensitive opportunity surface it produces.

**Source group:** writing bank §10 is the drafting base; INDEX_06 ("Structural Labor Supply," "RURO Estimation"), INDEX_03 ("Random-Utility Welfare"), INDEX_05 ("Shorrocks/Shapley").

**Overclaim warning:** do not state or imply that any single source already performs the full three-layer exercise. The integration is the JMP's; the sources are component anchors. Do not describe the opportunity object as "random" — opportunities are deterministic estimated feasible sets in the JMP welfare/decomposition layer (welfare spec V3-5).

**Paragraph plan:** one paragraph, three sentences: (1) the JMP's one-object-three-layers structure; (2) the literature supplies the layers separately, with the three anchor groups named; (3) the gap is integration, which is the contribution. This paragraph is the review's thesis and is referenced forward from §14.

## 5. Literature block 1: latent jobs and constrained opportunities

**Claim cluster:**
- C1.1 The empirical unit is a latent job package — a feasible (wage, hours, characteristics) bundle plus non-work — not a point on a common hours grid.
- C1.2 The latent-jobs approach is the model-class *departure* from fixed-grid labour supply; the fixed grid is the benchmark and foil, not an opportunity-density source.
- C1.3 Constrained choice sets matter empirically because omitted restrictions are absorbed into estimated preferences and elasticities.
- C1.4 A common hours grid cannot carry the JMP's household-specific access channel.

**Source group:**
- Latent-jobs architecture: Dagsvik & Jia 2016 [explicit]; Dagsvik et al. 2014 [background/manifesto]; Capeau & Decoster 2016 [explicit]; Aaberge & Colombino 2013 [explicit]; Aaberge & Colombino 2018 [background].
- Fixed-grid foil: Van Soest 1995 [explicit]; Bargain et al. 2013 [explicit, fixed-common-grid welfare comparator]; Aaberge et al. 2009 [explicit choice-set-representation comparison].
- Absorption-into-preferences motivation: Bargain et al. 2013 [explicit]; Van Soest 1995 [explicit availability-constraint effect]; Beffy et al. 2019 [explicit].

**Source tags / index basis:** INDEX_02 "Latent Jobs," "Constrained Choice Sets," "Relationship To Fixed-Grid Labor Supply Models"; writing bank §1.

**Overclaim warning:** these sources justify the latent-jobs architecture only. They do not justify the JMP's W¹–W⁶ welfare family or the three-way decomposition. Van Soest is the foil, not an opportunity-density source. A fixed grid is not generically "wrong"; it is insufficient for a paper whose object is unequal feasible sets. Most of these are two-way (preference/opportunity) sources — do not read the three-way access/ability/preference cut into them.

**Paragraph plan:** two paragraphs. P1: define the latent job as the empirical unit and place it against the fixed grid (C1.1, C1.2). P2: the empirical stakes — omitted constraints inflate apparent preference heterogeneity, motivating an explicit opportunity object (C1.3, C1.4), forward-linking to block 3's offer-side evidence and to the JMP's identification discipline.

## 6. Literature block 2: structural labour supply and RURO estimation

**Claim cluster:**
- C2.1 The JMP sits in the discrete-choice structural labour-supply tradition and extends it with an explicit, estimated opportunity density.
- C2.2 RURO estimation separates a preference block from an opportunity density and fits labour supply as choice among latent jobs.
- C2.3 In a single cross-section the preference/opportunity separation rests on functional form, exclusion/budget-set variation, and recovery/validation evidence — it is parametric and model-conditional, not nonparametric or instrument-free causal identification.
- C2.4 Couples are modelled as a joint/unitary decision unit, matching the JMP household welfare unit; intra-household distribution is out of scope.

**Source group:**
- Tradition/synthesis: Van Soest 1995 [explicit benchmark]; Aaberge & Colombino 2018 [background synthesis]; Aaberge & Colombino 2013 [explicit].
- RURO estimation: Dagsvik & Jia 2016 [explicit]; Capeau et al. 2015 [explicit estimation recipe]; Capeau & Decoster 2016 [explicit]; Aaberge & Colombino 2013 [explicit].
- Cross-section identification: Dagsvik & Jia 2016 [explicit]; Beffy et al. 2019 [explicit budget-constraint identification + Monte Carlo recovery]; Capeau & Decoster 2016 [explicit exclusion-restriction precedent]; JMP project state v1 [explicit synthetic-recovery gate].
- Unitary couples: Van Soest 1995, Capeau & Decoster 2016, Aaberge & Colombino 2013, Jacquet et al. 2026 [all explicit]; JMP project state v1 [explicit].

**Source tags / index basis:** INDEX_06 "Structural Labor Supply," "RURO Estimation," "Couples/Singles"; writing bank §10.

**Overclaim warning:** structural-labour-supply heritage does not imply that fixed-grid models contain opportunity densities. Most RURO sources have a two-way preference/opportunity split; the JMP's access/ability/preference cut is finer and is the JMP's own. Identification is parametric/model-conditional — do not present it as nonparametric or causal. Unitary-couples precedent is not evidence that within-couple welfare is identified; the JMP defers intra-household distribution as a named boundary.

**Paragraph plan:** two paragraphs. P1: the RURO factorization and where the JMP's engine sits in the tradition (C2.1, C2.2). P2: the honest identification statement — what does the work in a cross-section, and why the JMP adds a synthetic-recovery gate on top of in-sample fit (C2.3), closing with the unitary-household unit and its explicit boundary (C2.4). P2 should foreshadow the gender-split lesson (project state §3.7) without narrating it in full.

## 7. Literature block 3: constrained offers, hours restrictions, and occupation access

**Claim cluster:**
- C3.1 Observed hours are the joint product of preferences and a demand-side offer structure; hours in an offer are often employer-set, and involuntary non-participation is governed by an offer-arrival mechanism distinct from preferences.
- C3.2 Hours packages are not free continuous choices: empirical hours-offer distributions concentrate at institutional standard hours, far more than desired-hours distributions.
- C3.3 Omitting the offer side inflates estimated preference heterogeneity; making the opportunity mechanism richly covariate-dependent in a cross-section can collapse preference identification — the canonical precedent for the JMP's recovery discipline.
- C3.4 Occupation belongs in the access/opportunity layer (`loc4`), not in utility and not in the structural wage return; industry (`lindi`, NACE) is reserved for robustness. This is a design discipline and robustness frontier, not a claim inherited from the literature.

**Source group:**
- Offer constraints / arrival: Bloemen 2008 [explicit; *JoLE* 26(1):137–179]; Bloemen 2000 [explicit; **DP version; journal pages TBC**].
- Hours-offer concentration: Bloemen 2008 [explicit; 40h offer probability ≈0.55–0.57 — Dutch, not transportable].
- Preference-variance inflation / identification collapse: Bloemen 2008 [explicit]; Bloemen 2000 [explicit; **DP version; journal pages TBC**]; JMP project state v1 [explicit synthetic-recovery gate].
- Occupation-as-access: JMP project state v1 [explicit]; JMP welfare spec v5 [explicit]. Absences/conflations in literature: Dagsvik & Jia 2016, Capeau & Decoster 2016, Beffy et al. 2019 [explicit absences]; Aaberge & Colombino 2013 [explicit conflation warning].

**Source tags / index basis:** INDEX_02 "Random Opportunities," "Constrained Choice Sets," "Identification — Separating Preferences From Opportunities," "Occupation Availability"; INDEX_06 "Wage-Offer Modelling"; writing bank §§3, 11, 12, 13.

**Overclaim warning:** Bloemen 2000/2008 contain no money-metric welfare, no W¹–W⁶, no Shapley decomposition, and no proposal-corrected RURO likelihood; their offers arrive stochastically (Poisson), whereas the JMP's feasible sets are deterministic — state the analogy, not an isomorphism. Do not use Dutch magnitudes (40h probability, ~8pp participation gap) as numbers for France; they are plausibility benchmarks only. The Bloemen 2000 identification-collapse result pertains to a covariate-dependent Poisson intensity; cite it as historical precedent for the recovery gate, not as proof the JMP baseline is identified — that rests on the JMP's own synthetic recovery and the documented gender-split rejection. **No accepted source estimates an occupation-as-access object**; write C3.4 as design discipline. Bloemen 2008's education field/type covariate is neither occupation (`loc4`) nor industry (`lindi`) — carry no implication for the JMP occupation channel. Flag every Bloemen 2000 citation `[DP version; journal pages TBC]`.

**Paragraph plan:** three paragraphs. P1: the offer-side claim and its two layers — employer-set hours and offer-arrival non-participation (C3.1, C3.2). P2: the consequence for modelling — offer omission inflates preference heterogeneity, and over-flexible opportunity collapses identification, motivating the recovery gate (C3.3). P3: occupation-as-access as the JMP's own design extension, with the literature's absence/conflation stated honestly (C3.4).

## 8. Literature block 4: welfare in random utility and money-metric evaluation

**Claim cluster:**
- C4.1 The JMP welfare layer is downstream of a random-utility job-choice model but converts attained utility to money rather than stopping at choice probabilities.
- C4.2 Welfare must respect heterogeneous preferences — each household evaluated against its own indifference map — not raw income.
- C4.3 The baseline welfare object is ex ante: attained utility is the expected-maximum (inclusive value) over the feasible set; ex-post realised-job welfare cannot carry the access channel and is retained only as a diagnostic.
- C4.4 CV/EV under nonlinear-in-income random utility require expenditure-function/inversion machinery, not the linear-in-income log-sum shortcut.
- C4.5 For an opportunity paper, welfare must be computed over the household's feasible set, because access changes attained utility under every measure.

**Source group:**
- RUM welfare machinery: Dagsvik & Karlström 2005 [explicit; **DOI/JSTOR metadata pending**]; Bhattacharya 2015 [explicit; **DOI/JSTOR pending**]; Bhattacharya 2018 [background].
- Preference-respecting money metric: Bargain et al. 2013 [explicit]; Decancq et al. 2015 [explicit]; Fleurbaey & Maniquet 2018 JEL [explicit; **summary key provisional**]; Fleurbaey & Maniquet 2018 IJET [explicit supplementary].
- Ex-ante inclusive value: JMP welfare spec v5 [explicit]; JMP project state v1 [explicit]; Dagsvik & Karlström 2005 [derived-by-analogy for analytic-in-shocks machinery]; Aaberge & Colombino 2013 [background contrast — declines the expected-maximum route].
- Constrained-set welfare: JMP welfare spec v5, JMP project state v1 [explicit]; Dagsvik & Karlström 2005 [derived-by-analogy]; Jacquet et al. 2026 [background constrained-choice CV; **SSRN/DOI pending**].

**Source tags / index basis:** INDEX_03 "Random-Utility Welfare," "Inclusive Value," "Compensating/Equivalent Variation," "Money-Metric Welfare," "Welfare Under Constrained Opportunities"; writing bank §5.

**Overclaim warning:** Bargain and Jacquet are comparators, not sources for the JMP's six-measure level object. Dagsvik & Karlström supply CV/EV inversion machinery, not the JMP's ex-ante constrained-set level object and not opportunity-density formation. CV/EV are secondary cross-check forms in the JMP, not the primary level family. Do not attribute the ex-ante constrained-set object to the JEL/IJET theory papers — the ex-ante set valuation is the JMP's empirical construction.

**Paragraph plan:** two paragraphs. P1: the money-metric, preference-respecting object and its lineage (C4.1, C4.2, C4.4). P2: the ex-ante stance and why an opportunity paper must value the feasible set, not the realised job (C4.3, C4.5), forward-linking to block 5 (reference choice as the normative lever).

## 9. Literature block 5: responsibility, equivalent income, and equality of opportunity

**Claim cluster:**
- C5.1 The normative core is a compensation–responsibility distinction: some inequalities are compensation-relevant, others may be treated as responsibility-relevant.
- C5.2 Equivalent income is the literature's bridge between heterogeneous preferences and comparable welfare levels; the reference (wage/labour/preference/set) encodes the ethical stance.
- C5.3 The standard responsibility framing contrasts circumstances with preferences/effort; the JMP refines this into **access, ability, and preference**.
- C5.4 Ability (wage technology) is a *contested* channel — readable as responsibility-relevant productivity or compensation-relevant circumstance — so the JMP reports an access-only and an access-plus-ability bracket.
- C5.5 Access is the side the household does not fully control: market participation, hours availability, region/year environment, and occupation availability.
- C5.6 Preferences remain respected in the welfare metric even though preference is later treated as a decomposition source.

**Source group:**
- Compensation/responsibility lineage: Fleurbaey & Maniquet 2018 JEL [explicit]; Decancq et al. 2015 [explicit]; Bargain et al. 2013 [explicit]; Jacquet et al. 2026 [explicit comparator].
- Equivalent income / reference choice: Decancq et al. 2015, Bargain et al. 2013, Fleurbaey & Maniquet 2018 JEL/IJET [explicit].
- Three-way refinement: JMP welfare spec v5, JMP project state v1 [explicit]; Fleurbaey & Maniquet 2018 JEL [explicit Roemer/circumstance framing]; Capeau & Decoster 2016 [explicit two-way predecessor].
- Ability contested: JMP welfare spec v5 [explicit]; Fleurbaey & Maniquet 2018 JEL [explicit compensate-skill vs own-skill axis]; Dagsvik & Jia 2016, Capeau & Decoster 2016 [wage objects, derived-by-analogy].

**Source tags / index basis:** INDEX_04 all sections; writing bank §§4, 6.

**Overclaim warning:** these sources do not all define the same responsibility boundary — use as lineage and comparator, not identical models. The two-way (circumstance/preference or opportunity/preference) sources do not contain the JMP's three-way access/ability/preference cut; do not read it into them. Jacquet et al.'s reference-preference CV and Aaberge–Colombino common utility are preference-*neutralised* comparators, not the JMP's preference-*respecting* measure. The JEL paper is silent on access and does not identify wage technology econometrically. Do not attribute companion-theory-paper axioms to any published source; W¹–W⁶ characterisation, when discussed, cites the companion paper.

**Paragraph plan:** three paragraphs. P1: compensation–responsibility and equivalent income as the normative bridge, with reference choice as the lever (C5.1, C5.2). P2: the JMP's three-way refinement and why ability is the contested channel that earns a reported bracket (C5.3, C5.4). P3: access as the non-responsibility side including occupation, and the preference-respecting/preference-as-source distinction (C5.5, C5.6). This block must explicitly set up the W¹–W⁶ surface as the JMP's design (cross-ref §14 and welfare spec), not as a literature object.

## 10. Literature block 6: Shapley decomposition and source attribution

**Claim cluster:**
- C6.1 The decomposition should be Shapley–Shorrocks because it is exhaustive and order-independent; order dependence is precisely why marginal contributions are averaged over orderings.
- C6.2 The JMP decomposes inequality in the constructed welfare vector Ω^k, not observed income; the sources are structural blocks (access/ability/preference) neutralised/equalised and the welfare distribution recomputed — not additive money components.
- C6.3 Shorrocks 1982 is both foundation and warning: unaxiomatized factor decompositions can be arbitrary, and its covariance rule for additive income sources does not transport directly to structural channels.
- C6.4 The exactness gate: Shapley components must sum exactly to total inequality I(Ω^k) for each measure.
- C6.5 The decomposition is model-conditional and not causal; it does not identify channels by itself — structural identification comes from the RURO model and synthetic recovery.

**Source group:**
- Shapley/Shorrocks rule: Shorrocks 2013 [explicit]; Audoly et al. 2025 [explicit practitioner recipe]; Sastre & Trannoy 2002 [explicit; **DOI pending**]; Shorrocks 1982 [explicit; **DOI/JSTOR pending**].
- JMP object: JMP welfare spec v5, JMP project state v1 [explicit].
- Channel identification comes from elsewhere: Dagsvik & Jia 2016, Beffy et al. 2019 [explicit]; Shorrocks 2013 [background — decomposition rule only].

**Source tags / index basis:** INDEX_05 all sections; writing bank §7.

**Overclaim warning:** these sources justify the allocation *rule*, not the economic channel *partition* — the access/ability/preference cut is the JMP's identifying normative assumption. Order-independence of the rule does not make the partition causal or data-free. Do not cite Shorrocks 1982 as if it directly decomposes structural models; its covariance rule is for additive income sources. Symmetric interaction allocation is a convention with axiomatic backing, not evidence that channels are independent. Do not write "access causes X percent" — always carry the model-conditional qualifier. Shapley solves allocation/order dependence, not structural identification.

**Paragraph plan:** two paragraphs. P1: why Shapley–Shorrocks for an interacting-channel allocation problem, with the order-dependence rationale (C6.1, C6.4). P2: what the JMP decomposes (Ω^k, structural blocks) and the two firm caveats — Shorrocks 1982 as warning not transport, and the non-causal/identification-from-elsewhere point (C6.2, C6.3, C6.5).

## 12. Literature block 8: inference, simulation error, and clustered uncertainty

**Claim cluster:**
- C8.1 Cluster-robust inference on the household identifier (`idorighh`) is required: pooled cross-section observations from the same household share correlated errors, biasing default SEs downward; with ≈9,657 clusters the JMP is in the many-clusters regime where the standard CRVE and pairs bootstrap are well-behaved.
- C8.2 Headline uncertainty uses a cluster-robust bootstrap, also because bound/pinned parameters invalidate simple asymptotic SEs for headline welfare/decomposition claims.
- C8.3 Wage exogeneity is a consequential maintained assumption for the ability channel: labour-supply/welfare estimates are sensitive to wage imputation and the wage-preference independence assumption, motivating a first-order robustness axis.
- C8.4 Sampled-alternative estimation and ex-ante welfare integration create a separate integration-quality problem (effective sample size, redraw stability); the literature gives precedents but not the JMP's production certification.

**Source group:**
- Cluster-robust inference: Cameron & Miller 2015 [explicit; **canonical DOI not yet added**]; JMP project state v1, JMP welfare spec v5 [explicit].
- Wage exogeneity: Löffler et al. 2014 [explicit; IZA DP 8281 / SOEPpapers 675; published descendant `loefflerSensitivityStructuralLabor2018` at T3].
- Sampling/integration: Aaberge et al. 2009 [explicit sampled estimator]; Capeau & Decoster 2016 [explicit]; Aaberge & Colombino 2013 [background]; JMP project state v1 [explicit ESS/redraw frontier]; Dagsvik & Karlström 2005 [explicit analytic-in-shocks machinery]; Train 2009 [methods reference, simulation language only].

**Source tags / index basis:** INDEX_07 "Bootstrap," "Standard Errors," "Sampled Alternatives," "Simulation Error," "Simulated Likelihood"; INDEX_06 "Wage-Offer Modelling"; writing bank §§14, 15.

**Overclaim warning:** Cameron & Miller is an inference survey with no contact with structural labour supply, opportunity densities, or welfare — cite only for clustered-data inference. "Two-way clustering" in C&M is two error-covariance dimensions, not the JMP's three channels — unrelated terms; do not let the coincidence leak into the text. The few-clusters corrections (wild bootstrap as necessity, CR2/CR3) are background given ≈9,657 clusters. Löffler et al. is not RURO/latent-jobs and implements no opportunity-offer density — apply the wage-exogeneity caution by analogy to the ability channel; the ~0.2→~0.6 elasticity result is single-females-only (Germany/SOEP 2007), not generalisable to France. Dagsvik & Karlström do not solve the alternative-sampling ESS problem. Train 2009 is a simulation-methods reference only, not a welfare or decomposition source. The production ESS/redraw certification is the JMP's own gate, not settled by any source.

**Paragraph plan:** two paragraphs. P1: clustered uncertainty and the cluster-robust bootstrap, with the many-clusters justification and the bound-parameter rationale (C8.1, C8.2). P2: wage exogeneity as a first-order robustness axis for the ability channel, and the integration-quality problem as a JMP-specific frontier with literature precedents (C8.3, C8.4).

## 14. What the JMP adds

**Contribution claims (drafting base for the review's closing paragraph and a back-reference for the introduction):**
- A1 **Integration:** one estimated opportunity density carried consistently through estimation, ex-ante money-metric welfare over feasible sets, and Shapley–Shorrocks decomposition — a combination no source performs.
- A2 **The three-way cut:** refinement of the standard circumstance/preference (and the RURO two-way opportunity/preference) split into access, ability, and preference, with ability isolated as the contested wage-technology channel and reported as a bracket.
- A3 **The responsibility-sensitivity surface:** computing the whole W¹–W⁶ family rather than selecting one measure, so the inequality picture's movement along the compensation–responsibility spectrum is itself the empirical answer to the responsibility-disagreement question (welfare spec V3-1/V3-2).
- A4 **Occupation-as-access:** occupation (`loc4`) placed in the access layer, not utility or the structural wage return — a design extension with no direct literature precedent (`lindi`/NACE reserved for robustness).
- A5 **Identification discipline:** synthetic-recovery certification as the standard of evidence above in-sample fit and LR rejection (the gender-split lesson).

**Source basis:** JMP project state v1 §§1.3, 2.2, 6.1; JMP welfare spec v5 §0, V3-1/V3-2/V3-3; writing bank §§6, 10.

**Overclaim warning:** every "adds" claim is a contribution of *integration and design*, not a claim that the components are new.
